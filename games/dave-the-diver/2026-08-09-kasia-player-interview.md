Kasia isn't a designer — she's my partner, and she's about 20+ hours into
[[dave-the-diver|Dave the Diver]] on Switch. I recorded a short interview instead of
just watching her play, because I wanted her actual reasoning, not my read of her
face. What follows is my distillation; the full cleaned transcript is below.

1. **The grind pays for itself twice** — fish and kill sharks → the restaurant
   grows → money buys more gear → she dives again. She's explicit that a grind
   without a payoff loop attached would lose her.
   > "I get something out of the grind. It's not for nothing." — Kasia

2. **A dead shark needs a story, not just loot** — the game hands her money and
   ingredients for a kill; she supplies the actual meaning on top of that. The
   mechanical reward wasn't sufficient by itself — she wrote her own justification
   for it.
   > "I tell myself I saved the other fishes from the sharks." — Kasia

3. **No difficulty setting is a feature to her, not a gap** — she's never died,
   never wants to redo an encounter, and considers that the correct choice for
   what she's playing the game *for*. This isn't "the game is too easy," it's "I
   would quit if it weren't."
   > "I don't want to suddenly be suffering and redoing something 50 times and
   > losing interest. I play this game for joy." — Kasia

4. **She can feel the seam between two different levels of craft** — unprompted,
   she called the character art and cutscene animation "brilliant" and the
   underwater level design "cheap" — a plain seabed next to genuinely great
   pixel-art character work. Worth comparing directly against how
   [[world-building]] is handled in games that keep one consistent register
   throughout.

5. **Music off, sound on — and the sound is doing real work** — she plays with
   music at 20% and effects at 80%. The score reads as repetitive to her; the
   diegetic combat cues (her words: "bloom bloom bloom, then a shark") are the
   part she actually relies on to read a fight, even with no HUD difficulty to
   fall back on. Connects to how [[audio-feedback]] gets discussed on the
   sound-design side of the atlas.

6. **Dialogue has a length ceiling for her, not a quality threshold** — short
   story beats are fine regardless of writing quality; long conversations lose
   her regardless of writing quality. It's a duration problem, not a taste
   problem.
   > "If it's short, if it's a few messages, then it's fine. But when I have to
   > click through conversations for 10 minutes, I'm done." — Kasia

### New threads

**Why did you buy Dave the Diver, and what made you want it after I'd already
played it once myself?**
It's a mix of everything I love in gaming — grinding, fishing, swimming,
exploring the terrain.

**What other game would you compare it to?**
Stardew Valley and Dredge. Nothing beats Stardew Valley, that has to be said.
But what I like about this one is that it's funny — it has those funny
animations in between, and it gives the story a bit more character. I do find
it annoying when the bubbles get in the way of the touch controls, though.

**You said you love the grind. What makes a grind feel worth it to you, and
what makes this one specifically enjoyable?**
First of all, you keep fighting the sharks, so you're not just collecting for
nothing — you're killing them. In my head I tell myself I'm saving the other
fish from the sharks, so I get to enjoy the Diablo-style smashing and killing.
Then I have the second thread of making my restaurant better, feeding people,
making more money, buying more stuff. It's well balanced — I get something out
of the grind, it's not for nothing.

**Would you say the loop — diving, fishing, and running the restaurant — is
done well specifically because it's a combination? Would either half work on
its own?**
I think you could make an interesting fishing game on its own — the combination
enhances it, gives it a loop, but I wouldn't say it wouldn't work without the
other piece.

**You're an artist — what do you think of the art style?**
A little uneven. The pixel character art and the animated cutscene-style
sequences are brilliant, absolute mastery. But the level design underwater
feels cheap in comparison — very basic, nothing I haven't seen before. It would
be much more enjoyable if the underwater art matched the level the pixel
artists are working at.

**How's the writing? Do you engage with the story, or play purely for the
grind?**
I'm a good person to ask — I hate having to click through dialogue. Ninety
percent of games have boring dialogue you just click through, and it's the
same here in principle, but if it's short and it's only a few messages, it's
fine. When I have to click through conversations for ten minutes, I'm done. I
just want to grind.

**Is Dave the Diver a funny game?**
There are genuinely funny moments — characters have real personality, like the
gunsmith and his odd side comments. The animations between segments are funny.
The actual fishing itself, normally not — but there are funny little extras
worked in, partly to unlock story beats, partly to bring some challenge into
the grind.

**There's a combat component too — what do you think of it?**
I love it. It's easy enough that I've never died — not effortless, you still
spend time on it, air and resources matter — but it's a challenge in the
moment, not a punishing one. I never have to redo something ten times.

**So you're happy with the default difficulty — there's no difficulty
setting.**
Yes. I don't want to suddenly be suffering and redoing something fifty times
and losing interest. I play this for joy, so I want it a little easier — cozy
gaming, where you just keep moving forward.

**What about the sound design?**
Not the right person to ask about music — I always turn the music down to 20%
and the game sounds up to 80%, so I barely hear the score, and I find it
repetitive when I do. But the sound effects on their own are genuinely nice —
that rhythmic cue building up, then a shark shows up, then the combat sounds
kick in. That part works.

**What's the right price for this game, in your opinion?**
No idea what I actually paid — it was on sale. If I had to pay full price, up
to 20. But you can't really ask me that until I've finished it — if it turns
out to need 400 hours to finish, maybe it's worth 60; if I'm close to the end,
20 is right. I don't think a game is worth more just because I put more hours
into it — if it's a small story that didn't take as much work, it's worth less
than one that clearly had more work put into it.

**How many hours have you played?**
No idea exactly — 20-something on the Switch.

**Are you as motivated to keep playing as when you started?**
Yes, though part of that is I don't have anything else to play right now —
that's usually how it goes for me, I finish one thing and move to the next.
But as long as it's the one game I'm playing, I'm genuinely excited.
